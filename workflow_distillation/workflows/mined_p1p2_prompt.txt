## 2. Methodology

1. **Inspected input files.** `exposure_telomere_length_snps.tsv` contains 11 genome-wide significant leukocyte-telomere-length (LTL) SNPs from a published European-ancestry GWAS, each annotated with the telomere-*lengthening* ("long") allele and its effect in kb (`beta_kb`), but no standard error for the exposure effect. The 11 SNPs map to 10 distinct loci (TERC, TERT×2, NAF1, OBFC1, PXK, ZNF208, RTEL1, ZNF676, CTC1, ACYP2); two SNPs (rs2736100, rs7726159) both lie in the *TERT* gene region ~4 kb apart and are almost certainly correlated (LD), which I flagged as a judgement-relevant issue. The three outcome files (`outcome_lung_all.tsv`, `outcome_lung_adenocarcinoma.tsv`, `outcome_lung_squamous.tsv`) give log-odds betas, SEs, ORs, and allele information per SNP from lung cancer case-control GWAS; the overall-cancer file is missing rs2736100 (10/11 SNPs present), the two histology files have all 11.

2. **Harmonised alleles.** For each SNP I aligned the outcome effect allele to the exposure "long" allele: if they matched, I kept the outcome beta as is; if the outcome effect allele was the other allele, I flipped the sign of the outcome beta (and complemented the allele frequency for the record). This ensures every outcome beta represents the log-odds change per copy of the telomere-lengthening allele, matching the direction of `beta_kb`. Because `beta_kb` is already expressed per kb of telomere length (not per allele-SD), the per-SNP Wald ratio (`beta_outcome / beta_kb`) directly gives a log-OR **per 1 kb** of telomere length — no further rescaling was needed.

3. **Chose an MR estimator compatible with missing exposure SEs.** Since no SEs are given for `beta_kb`, I used the standard two-sample summary-MR formulas that only require the outcome SE for inverse-variance weighting (Burgess-style IVW: β = Σ(βx·βy·w)/Σ(βx²·w), w = 1/SEy², which implicitly assumes no measurement error in the exposure effect — a standard simplification when exposure SEs are unavailable). I computed this fixed-effect IVW for each outcome, then additionally computed Cochran's Q and I² to test instrument heterogeneity (a proxy for horizontal pleiotropy given the small, curated SNP panel).

4. **Primary estimate: random-effects (overdispersion-corrected) IVW.** Because Q was significant for the overall-cancer (I²=71%) and adenocarcinoma (I²=88%) analyses, I inflated the fixed-effect SE by √(Q/df) (multiplicative random-effects IVW), which is the conventional correction used in MR when instruments show heterogeneity. This is reported as the primary estimate in the Results table; point estimates are identical to fixed-effect IVW, but confidence intervals are appropriately widened.

5. **Pleiotropy-robust sensitivity analyses.** (a) Weighted-median MR (weights = inverse variance of each Wald ratio, approximated using only outcome SE; 95% CI from 5,000 parametric bootstrap draws of βy ~ N(β̂y, SEy)) — robust to up to ~50% invalid instruments. (b) MR-Egger weighted regression of βy on βx (weights 1/SEy²) to test for directional pleiotropy via the intercept. (c) Leave-one-out and an explicit sensitivity analysis dropping the two *TERT*-locus SNPs.

6. **Checked for LD/pleiotropy at the TERT locus.** Inspecting per-SNP Wald ratios showed the two TERT SNPs gave outlying, much larger per-kb ORs (≈3.4–9.8) than all other loci (≈0.8–2.7) in the overall and adenocarcinoma analyses — the classic, well-documented signature of TERT variants influencing lung cancer through pathways other than telomere length (horizontal pleiotropy), compounded by likely LD between the two SNPs violating the independent-instruments assumption. **Decision:** I kept all 11 SNPs in the primary (conservative, literature-standard) analysis but ran an explicit TERT-excluded IVW sensitivity analysis. Removing TERT substantially reduced heterogeneity (I² 71%→29% for overall cancer; Q no longer significant) and reduced but did not remove the adenocarcinoma association (OR 3.05→2.13, still p<10⁻¹⁶) or the squamous null (OR 0.96→0.93, still null). 7. **MR-Egger intercept test.** Intercept p-values were non-significant for all three outcomes (p=0.77–0.97), providing no strong evidence of *unbalanced* directional pleiotropy, though statistical power is low with only 10–11 instruments; this does not rule out balanced/correlated pleiotropy from the TERT locus already identified in step 6.

8. **Converted to OR scale.** Final log-odds estimates and their CIs were exponentiated to report odds ratios per 1 kb longer telomere length.

Genetically predicted longer leukocyte telomere length is causally associated with **increased risk of lung cancer overall** (IVW OR≈1.58 per kb, 95% CI 1.26–1.97, Step 4) and this effect is **concentrated in, and much stronger for, lung adenocarcinoma** (OR≈3.05 per kb, 95% CI 1.99–4.67, Step 4), remaining strongly significant (OR≈2.1, p<10⁻¹⁶) even after removing the two potentially pleiotropic *TERT*-locus SNPs (Step 6) and under the pleiotropy-robust weighted-median method (OR≈2.10, Step 5). 4. **Primary MR: IVW of per-SNP Wald ratios.** For each outcome, computed each SNP's Wald ratio (`aligned outcome beta / beta_kb`) and its SE (`outcome SE / |beta_kb|`, ignoring exposure-side uncertainty — the standard "NOME" simplification, justified by the strong instruments from Step 3, but a real source of possible slight CI under-estimation). 5. **Pleiotropy-robust sensitivity: weighted median and MR-Egger.** Computed the simple weighted-median estimator (bootstrap SE, 10,000 resamples of each ratio ~ N(ratio, SE)) as an estimator robust to up to ~50% invalid-instrument weight. MR-Egger slopes had enormous SEs (e.g., HNC Egger CI 0.02–57) given only 6–7 points and 4–5 residual df; it is reported but explicitly **not** treated as a reliable pleiotropy test — its non-significant intercepts cannot be read as reassurance of "no pleiotropy," only as "underpowered to detect it."

9. **Ran leave-one-SNP-out analysis** for every outcome under the random-effects IVW model. For lung (overall) and lung adenocarcinoma, every single-SNP removal kept the OR >1 and p<0.025 — no one instrument drives the signal, and the heterogeneity in adenocarcinoma reflects generally dispersed (not single-outlier) effect sizes, consistent with possible balanced horizontal pleiotropy that the random-effects variance correction addresses but cannot fully rule out as a bias source. For squamous and HNC, all leave-one-out estimates remained non-significant and of similar magnitude, so the null findings are not hiding a suppressed signal from one SNP.

10. **Assembled final results** (step above) and considered: (a) substantial loss of TERT/CTC1/ZNF208 instruments (step 4) limiting pathway coverage; (b) unresolved residual heterogeneity for lung adenocarcinoma even after removing the palindromic SNP (Qp 0.00087→0.020), meaning the IVW "no net pleiotropy" assumption is stressed and the point estimate, while directionally and quantitatively consistent across methods, is not immune to bias from pleiotropy; (c) multiple outcomes tested (4) without formal multiple-testing correction — only lung overall and lung adenocarcinoma are nominally significant, and this should temper confidence; (d) modest HNC sample sizes (~4,600–5,400 per set) giving wide CIs, so the HNC null should be read as inconclusive/underpowered rather than a confirmed absence of effect.
## 3. Conclusion

Using the available genome-wide-significant leukocyte telomere length (LTL) instruments that could be reliably harmonized to each outcome (Methodology steps 4–6), genetically longer LTL is associated with **higher risk of lung cancer overall** (OR≈1.47/kb, step 8) and, most strongly and robustly, **higher risk of lung adenocarcinoma** (OR≈2.15/kb, steps 8–9), with directionally and quantitatively concordant results from weighted median and leave-one-out analyses (step 9) despite some residual between-SNP heterogeneity (steps 7–8) that cannot be fully excluded as reflecting pleiotropy. There is **no evidence of association with lung squamous cell carcinoma** (OR≈0.92/kb, step 8), a consistent null across all sensitivity analyses (steps 8–9). For **head and neck (oral/pharyngeal) cancer**, the point estimate is in the direction of increased risk (OR≈1.37/kb) but the confidence interval is wide and crosses 1 (step 8); given the modest case numbers and the loss of the TERC instrument for this outcome (step 4), this result is best described as **inconclusive** rather than a confirmed null.

**Key limitations:** (i) only 6–7 of 11 candidate instruments could be used per outcome after conservative, LD-reference-free harmonization, dropping the TERT locus (two of the strongest instruments) entirely and TERC for HNC specifically — this likely understates the true effect and reduces power, especially for HNC; (ii) the palindromic SNP rs6772228 could not be strand-verified against exposure allele frequencies (none were provided), though results were robust to its exclusion; (iii) residual heterogeneity for lung adenocarcinoma was not resolved by any available method, so some contribution from unbalanced horizontal pleiotropy cannot be ruled out; (iv) MR-Egger was uninformative at this instrument count and should not be used to claim "no pleiotropy"; (v) no multiple-testing correction was applied across the four outcomes.
